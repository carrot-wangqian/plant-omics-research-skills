# Claims and chapter logic

Start with a one-sentence task for the chapter. When structuring a methods development chapter, separate requirements, method evaluation, and application to the target study. For a results chapter, order sections by the questions the evidence answers. End on what the chapter adds rather than listing every operation.

For complex drafting, keep a working table: claim, record and version, unit and contrast, panel, strength, issue. A sample or method discrepancy should show what each source says and which claims depend on it. A protocol is a method reference; only execution records establish what was actually done. Do not infer missing values from a reference, default, brochure, or screenshot.

| Evidence | Appropriate wording | Do not imply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A spatial feature with a mass-based candidate match | An ion feature is consistent with a candidate annotation | Unique molecular identity or absolute concentration |
| A cluster supported by multiple markers and anatomical context | A putative cell type or expression domain | Every spatial unit is one pure cell type |
| Agreement across registered adjacent sections | Regional spatial correspondence between sections | Cell-level overlap or evidence of a mechanism |
| A model-based association or developmental ordering | Association or inferred ordering under the model | Causality, elapsed time, or lineage tracing |
| An independently controlled validation result | A specified measurement supports that claim under tested conditions | Every downstream mechanistic step is established |

When describing MSI, distinguish detected ions, candidate annotations, reference-standard evidence, scaling, and independent replication. Do not compare colored intensities across inconsistent scales or count ion/adduct features as unique molecules without a justified rule. For single-cell and single-nucleus work, use the actual capture unit and sampling design; captured fractions are not automatically tissue composition. For spatial transcriptomics, identify the spot, bin or segmented unit and distinguish assay spacing from effective resolution. For integration, distinguish matched samples, adjacent sections, and stage-level comparisons. Report limits once where they affect interpretation.

## Synthetic revision

**Input:** Two images from neighboring sections of a fictional leaf show signal concentrated near the same margin. No same-section mapping was performed.

**Result:** "Both assays showed elevated signal near the leaf margin in adjacent sections."

**Discussion boundary:** "The regional agreement motivates a same-section comparison; the current data do not establish cell-level colocalization."

## Revision checks for annotation and spatial results

Structure candidate annotation by named evidence source or method, and report observed spatial distribution separately. Replace internal strategy codes with ordinary method names; report total eligible, newly matched and unresolved/conflicting features against the exact source versions and give each percentage its denominator. When sources disagree or rule sets changed, reconcile feature ID sets using a documented overlap and deduplication rule: newly matched, retained, lost and reclassified. Net change in a total is not evidence of gross new matches. A new match is not a new measurement or confirmed identity. State cross-group features and anatomical comparability, scale or normalization, and independent units before comparing patterns. Describe the observed result before proposing a physical, technical or biological explanation. Mark analyses present only in a plan as proposed rather than completed. Draw prose conventions from relevant primary papers without borrowing their data, claims or wording.
